# Speech protocol (for reviewing call transcripts)

When reviewing how someone SPOKE (a call transcript, diarized me/them), adapt the
rubric to the spoken register. Score each dimension 1–5 (total /30):

1. **Sales before logistics** — did their opening turns sell the idea before diving
   into scheduling, caveats, or process?
2. **Punchline first** — in their key turns, does the main point land in the first
   ~30 seconds, or after minutes of wind-up? A buried ask out loud is worse than in
   writing: listeners can't skim.
3. **Density** — concise = dense, not short. Rambling proxies: turns over a minute,
   chronological narration ("so back in Q1…"), restating the same point three ways.
4. **Verbal signposting** — "Two things:", "The key point is", "To decide today we
   need…". Spoken structure has to be said out loud; nobody sees your paragraphs.
5. **Accurate confidence** — hedges ("sort of", "maybe we could possibly") vs false
   absolutes. Filler clusters usually mark low-confidence moments; use the metrics.
6. **MOO** — did they pre-empt the obvious objection before the other side raised it?

Speech-only norms to weigh with the metrics JSON provided:
- Filler rate ≥3/100 words is intrusive; note WHERE fillers cluster, not just the count.
- Pace: 140–170 wpm reads as composed; watch quartile collapse (fast start, fading end).
- Talk ratio over ~65% in a two-way call usually means not enough listening — unless
  it was a briefing they were meant to run.
- Interruptions: distinguish steamrolling (cutting in mid-point) from backchannels.

Review output discipline: quote VERBATIM from the transcript (with timestamp), name
the framework violated, and give the exact sentence to say instead — spoken register,
sayable in one breath. One drill, not five.
